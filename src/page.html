Five hours, one beach

The same stretch of Route 109 painted at five times of day, like Monet returning to his haystacks. Watch the light move across Mudkip, the water and the lighthouse.

    The fin on its head works like radar, reading currents in the water and air. The orange gills on its cheeks let it breathe underwater, which is why it never minds a wave.

    The palette

    Each part of Mudkip is shaded from its own five-step ramp, deepest shadow to highlight, then outlined a shade darker. Every painting regrades these ramps for its hour.

    The technique

    Mudkip is a small 3D model of ellipsoids and flat fins, cast straight into pixels with toon shading, cast shadows and hand-drawn eyes. That is why it can turn, jump and squash from any angle.

    The sea runs on a 6.6-second swash cycle: the wave breaks, rushes up the sand, leaves a line of foam and drains away. Splashes, ripples and reflections are drawn pixel by pixel on every frame.
